How to play RPS 10

Rock paper scissors rules, with a 10-card hand. Everything you need to know in about five minutes.

Play while you read

The basics

RPS 10 uses the classic rules of rock paper scissors:

  • Rock beats scissors (rock blunts scissors)
  • Scissors beats paper (scissors cut paper)
  • Paper beats rock (paper wraps rock)
  • The same card on both sides is a draw for that round.

The twist: instead of choosing freely every time, each player plays from a hand of 10 cards, and every card can only be played once.

Your hand: 3 + 3 + 4

At the start of a match you and your opponent are each dealt 10 cards: three of two kinds and four of the third. For example, 3 rock, 3 paper and 4 scissors. Your opponent knows your hand is split 3 + 3 + 4, but not which kind you hold four of, and you don't know theirs either.

During the match the game shows a small tracker, "Opp has", with the range of each kind your opponent can still be holding (for example rock 2–3). It updates as they play, so you always know the possibilities. Working out the rest is up to you.

Before the first round: arrange your hand

You get 20 seconds to look at your hand and drag the cards into any order you like. Tap Ready on the table when you're set; the match starts when both players are ready or the time runs out.

Each round: pick, lock in, reveal

  1. Pick a card from your hand (tap it, or press keys 1–0 on a keyboard).
  2. Lock in before the 10-second timer runs out. You can change your mind until you lock in.
  3. When both players have locked in, the cards are revealed together. Neither player can see the other's card before then.
  4. The winner of the round scores a point. Both cards are now gone from the hands.

A match is always 10 rounds, so every card gets played. The final round is special: by then each player has exactly one card left, and if you've been counting, you may already know what it is.

Winning the match

After 10 rounds, the player who won more rounds wins the match. If you won the same number of rounds, the match is a draw. Drawn rounds don't count for either side.

Running out of time

If you don't lock in within 10 seconds, what happens depends on the mode.
  • Ranked: you lose the round, and a random card from your hand is discarded.
  • All other modes: a random card from your hand is played for you, and the round is judged as normal.

If your connection drops, you have 15 seconds to come back before the match is given to your opponent.

If no one is found for an online match within about 15 seconds, you play a bot instead so you're never left waiting. Bots are always labelled.

Ranks and rating

Everyone starts with a rating of 1,000. Ranked wins raise it and losses lower it, by more or less depending on your opponent's rating. Ranks are Bronze (below 1,100), Silver (1,100), Gold (1,200) and Diamond (1,350). Repeat matches against the same player within 24 hours count for less each time, then not at all.

Seasons last a calendar month. At the start of a new season, last season's top player is crowned champion and every rating moves halfway back towards 1,000. A weekly board (3 points for a ranked win, 1 for a draw) resets every week.

Ranked matches give 1.5× XP and coins; Practice and bot matches give half. Your first win of the day adds 50 coins, the daily challenge gives 30 coins for playing and 30 more for a win, plus 25 bonus XP. XP fills a 50-level road of unlocks, and coins buy card styles, avatars and more in the Store.

The daily challenge

Every day at midnight UTC there's a new challenge. Everyone is dealt the same hand and faces the same bot, which plays its cards in the same fixed order for everyone. You get one try a day, and leaving early counts as a loss. Play on back-to-back days to build a streak; seven days in a row earns the Week streak award.

Chat and fair play

You can send quick reactions and set phrases during and after a match (one every 3 seconds). There's no free typing, and you can mute your opponent's messages in Settings. The game server deals every hand and judges every round, so neither player can see the other's card early. Players can be reported for offensive names or stalling.

Ready?

That's everything. The best way to learn is a few practice rounds, then the strategy guide when you want to start winning more.
